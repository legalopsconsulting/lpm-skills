# RAG status methodology

The full methodology for assigning Red/Amber/Green status to workstreams and matters in status reports.

## Contents

1. The fundamental principle — RAG is judgment, not arithmetic
2. Definitions — Green, Amber, Red
3. Critical RAG judgment calls
4. The reasoning rule — always state why, not just the colour

---

## 1. The fundamental principle

RAG is judgment, not arithmetic. The status reflects the likelihood of the workstream or matter delivering its objectives on time and on budget — it is forward-looking, not a historical score.

A workstream that has delivered every milestone on time but has a major dependency unresolved next week is not Green. A workstream that has had setbacks but has a credible recovery plan and clear path to delivery is not Red.

The model the skill should apply: would an experienced LPM, looking at all the evidence, expect this workstream to deliver its objectives within the agreed parameters?

---

## 2. Definitions

### Green

On track to deliver within agreed scope, timeline, and budget. No unresolved issues that could derail delivery. Minor items being managed within normal tolerance.

### Amber

At risk of missing agreed parameters. One or more of:
- A dependency is unresolved
- Budget consumption is ahead of progress
- A key decision is pending
- A jurisdiction has gone quiet without explanation

Amber means "this needs attention to stay on track" — it requires a return-to-green plan explaining what specific actions will resolve the risk and by when.

### Red

Will not meet agreed parameters without intervention. Budget exceeded or will be exceeded, timeline blown, scope has changed without agreement, or a blocking issue has no resolution path. Red requires an escalation path and remediation plan — not just "it's late" but "here's what we're doing about it and here's the revised forecast."

## 3. Critical RAG judgment calls

These are the recurring judgment calls that distinguish a useful status report from a misleading one.

### Forward-looking vs current snapshot

A workstream at 95% of budget with 50% of work completed is **Red** even though budget isn't technically exceeded yet — the trajectory is unsustainable. Forward-looking assessment matters more than current snapshot.

### Sunk cost vs ongoing risk

A workstream at 110% of budget that's 100% complete is **Green** (or at most Amber for reporting purposes) — the overrun is sunk cost, the work is done. The question is whether the overrun has been addressed commercially (scope change recognised, client notified). The financial issue does not change the delivery status.

### Vagueness as default Amber

"On track" with no specifics is **Amber** by default — it's vague and may mask issues the team hasn't identified. Probe what "on track" means relative to the plan. If the team can produce a specific demonstration of progress against the plan, it can move to Green; if they can't, it stays Amber and the gap is reported.

### Silence as escalating concern

No update received for two or more reporting periods is **Amber** trending **Red** — silence is more concerning than bad news because bad news at least means someone is paying attention. The right approach is to report the silence as a status condition, not omit the workstream because no update was received.

In internal reports, silence is Amber regardless of jurisdiction predictability or local team track record. The latitude for established regulatory regimes described in Section 4 of the output templates applies to client-facing reports only — in an internal report, the partner needs to know that a workstream has gone quiet, even if the most likely explanation is benign.

### Client dependencies as automatic Amber

A dependency on client action (confirmation, approval, decision) is **Amber** from the moment it's identified until it's resolved — even if everything else is Green. Client dependencies are the single largest source of timeline risk.

### Overall programme status rollup

If any workstream is Red, overall is Red. If two or more workstreams are Amber, overall is Amber. If one workstream is Amber and all others are Green, overall is Amber unless the Amber is minor and explicitly time-bounded with a clear resolution action already in train. Green overall requires all workstreams at Green or confirmed-complete. "Green with Amber items" is not a valid overall RAG rating — it is an Amber programme. Pick one colour and state the reasoning.

### Dual RAG when status is split

Some workstreams have a process Green and a budget Amber, or vice versa. Don't force a single RAG when the underlying picture is split — present both and explain. France: Green for process (filings on track), Amber for budget (running 15% over due to additional regulatory queries).

---

## 4. The reasoning rule

Always state the reasoning behind RAG status, not just the colour. The colour without context is decorative; the reasoning is what makes the report useful.

**Useful:** "Germany: Amber — regulatory filing submitted but processing confirmation not yet received; expected 4-week window expires 15 March. If confirmation not received by 8 March, escalate to local counsel for status check."

**Not useful:** "Germany: Amber"

The reasoning must include three things:
- What the current position is
- Why that position warrants the colour
- What action or trigger date governs the next change

For Amber and Red, this extends to the recovery plan: what specifically resolves this, by when, and with what trigger if the resolution doesn't land.
